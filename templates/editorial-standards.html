Research and accuracy

We aim to verify material facts against the strongest available evidence, such as filings, official release notes, technical documentation and research. When an original source is unavailable or interested in the outcome, we look for independent context and attribute claims to the people or organisations making them. We clarify which dates, markets and product versions a claim applies to.

News, analysis and opinion

News accounts should identify what is confirmed and who confirmed it. Analysis explains what the evidence may mean. Opinion makes a reasoned argument. We aim to label those formats and identify forecasts, rumours and reported allegations wherever readers could mistake them for established facts.

Original work and attribution

StackedByBayo articles should add explanation, context or practical guidance. We link to sources so readers can follow the evidence. When we feature headlines from another publisher, we identify the publisher and link to the original. We credit reused images and identify illustrations or archival images where the distinction matters.

Reviews and benchmarks

A hands-on label is reserved for documented direct use. Research-based overviews describe the sources used and the limits of what they establish. We attribute manufacturer claims, disclose relevant conditions for any tests we publish, and avoid invented scores, measurements or experiences.

Conflicts of interest

We aim to disclose relationships that could affect how readers interpret coverage, including review loans, gifts, affiliate links and paid arrangements. Editorial conclusions should remain independent of commercial arrangements. See our disclosures.

Responsible use of AI

Our standard for any use of AI is editorial accountability. If an AI tool assists with research, drafting or editing, its output needs verification against reliable sources. It must never stand in for reporting, quotes or hands-on testing. We aim to disclose uses that materially affect how readers should interpret the work.

Corrections and updates

Even well-sourced stories can contain mistakes. Our corrections policy explains how to flag an error and how we document changes that affect a reader’s understanding.
