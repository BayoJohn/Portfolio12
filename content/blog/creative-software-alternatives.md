The right creative application depends on what you have to hand off. A flat image for a newsletter, layered artwork for a client and a video project someone else must edit place different demands on software. That is a better starting point than trying to replace a familiar application feature for feature.

## GIMP and Krita serve different jobs

GIMP is a raster image editor. It suits tasks such as compositing a photograph, retouching a layer and exporting web graphics. Krita is designed around painting and illustration, with brush and canvas tools intended for creating an image from scratch. Try a sample with your actual tablet, fonts and export dimensions. If a client requires an editable Photoshop file, ask them to check a sample in their usual app. Layered interchange, effects and text are more demanding than a flat export.

## Test the video handoff

Kdenlive offers a multi-track editing timeline and supports proxies, smaller working copies that can make editing large source files more manageable. Those features make it worth considering for a self-contained video. When another editor needs your timeline in a different application, check titles, transitions, timing and audio tracks in a short exchange first. Sending the finished video solves a different problem from sending an editable project.

## Choose the format with the fewest surprises

If you own the entire workflow, save in the editor’s native format and keep the original media. For shared projects, agree on a delivery format and a revision process before committing. The free tool may be ideal when the output is flexible. Keeping the existing tool can make sense when a collaborator, course or client depends on a specific project format.
