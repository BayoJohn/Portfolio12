The word “free” on a download button usually says something about the price. It tells you much less about the rights you get, whether the app will keep working without a subscription, or who supplied the copy. Check those questions separately when choosing software.

## Start with the licence and business model

The Open Source Definition describes rights including redistribution, access to source and the ability to distribute modifications under the licence terms. Free software uses a related idea about user freedoms. Proprietary freeware can still cost nothing while limiting modification or redistribution. A freemium service usually offers a basic tier and charges for extra features or capacity. Bitwarden is an example of why these labels can overlap: it publishes client source code and offers both free and paid hosted plans. Paid offerings can also include support or a packaged build of open-source software.

## Follow the publisher to the correct channel

Use the project’s website, a trusted operating-system repository or the store linked by the publisher. Check the app name and developer carefully, especially when an ad or search result resembles the official page. For macOS and Windows, keep the system’s installation protections enabled. If an unfamiliar download asks you to override a warning, go back to the publisher and check the specific file before you continue.

## Check the work the installer is asking to do

A password vault or a system utility may need permissions for a specific feature. A simple media player asking for access to unrelated data deserves a closer look. On mobile, review permissions and keep the system and app current. On all platforms, get updates from the same trusted channel where practical.

If the publisher provides a checksum or signature and you know how to verify it, get the expected value from the official source. Verification helps establish that you received the intended file. It is one part of checking whether you trust the publisher and whether the app fits your needs.
