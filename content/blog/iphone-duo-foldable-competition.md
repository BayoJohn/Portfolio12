Apple introduced the iPhone Duo in September and listed October 23 as the start of availability. The hardware is easy to describe: a compact outer screen and a larger one inside. The more consequential question is what happens when you open an app. A foldable earns its extra size and complexity every time a task gets easier on the larger screen.

## Two screens, one workflow

Apple specifies a 5.4-inch outer display and a 7.6-inch inner one. It highlights the screens’ shared aspect ratio and support for running apps side by side. Similar proportions can make the jump between screens feel familiar. It still takes work by developers to make the opened view worthwhile. An email client can use the extra space for the inbox and a message at once. An app that simply stretches its phone layout may leave you reading the same information across a wider line.

Samsung entered this cycle with a broader foldable lineup. Its July launch included the book-style Galaxy Z Fold8 range and the clamshell Galaxy Z Flip8. Those are different ways of using a hinge. One gives you room to work after you open it. The other is designed to take up less space when closed. That choice matters more than a comparison of diagonals alone.

## What buyers should look for after launch

Try the apps that would justify the purchase for you. Open a spreadsheet, switch between screens with a message half-written, or see whether a video app works well beside a second app. Check the warranty terms for the inner screen and hinge and ask where those parts can be serviced. The success of this generation will depend partly on how many apps turn a bigger display into a more useful one.
