Nvidia announced on November 23 that Jensen Huang had returned as president, CEO and a director after a unanimous board vote. The announcement resolves who runs the chipmaker. It also exposes the harder governance question: how will the directors oversee a powerful founder while their own investigation remains open?

## What the board has disclosed

Nvidia said the board reached its decision after discussions with Huang and received input from senior levels of the US government. The board said it was satisfied with Huang’s ability to lead and concluded that reinstatement posed no national-security risk. That is the board’s assessment. The company also said an independent committee assisted by outside counsel would continue its inquiry. Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, remains CFO, and Harvey Jones remains chair.

The Associated Press reported on November 24 that the decision had intensified scrutiny of the directors. Nvidia also announced an independent National Security and Export Control Committee. Its creation gives investors a named body to watch when questions arise about how export controls and national-security risks are handled.

## Why the distinction matters

Companies buying accelerators make infrastructure decisions that can last several years. A CEO’s return can give customers a clearer person to negotiate with, but customers also need predictable compliance practices, supply and software support. For shareholders, the questions are more specific: who oversees the investigation, what will the board disclose when it ends, and how does the company plan for another sudden leadership change?

The committee’s eventual findings will matter more than the tone of the reinstatement announcement. Until then, the public record establishes the board vote, the change of leadership and an investigation still in progress.
