Urgency is often the most important detail in a scam message. A text may say your bank account is about to be blocked. A WhatsApp voice note may sound like a relative in trouble. AI voice imitation can make a familiar voice less reliable as a cue. You can verify the request without deciding whether the message was made by a person or generated by software.

## Separate the identity from the request

For a person, call a number already in your contacts or check with someone who knows them. For a bank, open the banking app yourself or use the contact details published independently by the institution. A caller who asks for a PIN, an OTP or a WhatsApp registration code is asking for information that can authorise sensitive actions. Keep it private even if they know your name.

## If you were already persuaded to act

Contact the service through a verified channel. Explain what you shared or sent and when it happened. For a bank transfer, tell the bank the transaction details and ask about its fraud-reporting process. If a messaging account may be involved, review linked devices through the official app and use the platform’s support instructions.

## Make verification normal

A family can agree to confirm unusual requests on a second channel. A workplace can route payment changes through a known approver. These checks are easier to follow when people know that delaying an unusual request is expected. A polished message, a familiar voice and a convincing logo cannot substitute for an independently verified request.
