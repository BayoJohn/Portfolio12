Xiaomi’s third-quarter results, published November 24, give a concrete view of the memory squeeze from a phone maker’s side. The company said key component costs, including memory, continued to rise and that competition intensified. It reported shipping 30.7 million smartphones in the quarter. That figure describes shipments in a completed quarter. It does not establish how many devices Xiaomi or competitors Oppo and Vivo plan to ship in 2027.

## Why a company can change the phone before changing the price

Working memory and storage are both choices a handset maker makes before it sells the phone. A brand trying to protect a familiar price point might offer fewer storage variants or sell fewer models with generous base capacities. A manufacturer could also raise prices, reduce discounts, or shift its mix toward premium phones. These are possible responses to costs and competition, rather than decisions established by Xiaomi’s filing.

TrendForce’s September production outlook noted some buyers were bringing forward purchases amid concerns about higher prices. Earlier buying can distort the next comparison. A good quarter of sales in one region could come partly from people who would otherwise have replaced their phones months later.

## Follow configurations before drawing conclusions

For someone shopping in Nigeria, regional variants complicate the comparison. Confirm the actual RAM and storage listed on the invoice, whether the phone supports your carrier, and who handles warranty service. When comparing this year’s model with its successor, match the capacities and sale terms. The retail price on the front of the box only tells part of the story. The rest is in what you get at that price and what it costs to keep the device working.
