Security terms are most helpful when you know which question each one answers. An identifier, an incident type and a defensive practice can appear in the same headline. They describe different parts of the situation and suggest different responses.

## Vulnerabilities and incidents

A **CVE** provides a shared identifier for a publicly disclosed vulnerability. Use it to match a warning to the right vendor advisory. **Zero-day** commonly describes a vulnerability or attack for which defenders had no prior opportunity to patch when it was first used or disclosed. Check how a source is using the term. **Ransomware** interferes with access to data or systems and demands payment. In some incidents there is also data theft, which a backup cannot undo.

## Security work and evidence

**Endpoint detection and response**, or EDR, helps teams monitor activity on computers and investigate or contain suspicious events. A **security operations centre**, or SOC, is the function that monitors, investigates and coordinates responses. **Threat intelligence** turns evidence about threats into information a defender can act on. A useful briefing explains why the evidence changes a defensive decision.

## People and access

**Social engineering** manipulates a person into taking an action or giving up information. A second, independent channel can help verify unusual requests. The **principle of least privilege** limits access to what a user or program needs for a specific job.

## Least privilege

Do everyday work from an account with ordinary permissions when practical. Review who can change your home router’s settings and whether you still need remote administration. Check which phone apps can access contacts, location or the microphone. These are practical ways to reduce the consequences when a device or a single account is misused. The [Tech Academy guide to account and device protection](../../academy/protect-your-accounts-and-devices/) gives you a place to start.
