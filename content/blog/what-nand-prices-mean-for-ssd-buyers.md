A cloud company can reserve storage in volumes that a local computer shop will never see. Both ultimately buy NAND flash, but the price signals are different. In its September 30 forecast, TrendForce projected fourth-quarter NAND contract prices would rise 15–20% from the previous quarter and expected enterprise SSD price growth to accelerate. These were contract-market forecasts, not an index of consumer drives on shelves in November.

## Why the two markets move differently

Flash is the part of an SSD that stores data after the power goes off. Enterprise and consumer drives also include controllers and firmware. A manufacturer can sell into the enterprise market with products built for different workloads and service expectations. SK hynix reported growing sales of its high-layer-count enterprise SSDs in the third quarter. That helps explain why suppliers might direct more output and attention toward data centres.

A retailer may still be selling drives bought under older terms. It might discount an outgoing model while a new model arrives with a higher price. Comparing an enterprise flash contract with a familiar retail product therefore confuses both the product and the period being measured.

## How to read a price change

Track the same capacity and model number with the same warranty. Check whether the SSD is for a compatible slot and whether your work involves sustained writing. A fast peak-speed claim says little about how the drive behaves across a long file transfer. If you just need room for an archive, the amount of usable space and a reliable backup plan can matter more.

A forecast helps explain why an old promotion might be hard to replace. It cannot tell you what a drive will cost in your city next month.
