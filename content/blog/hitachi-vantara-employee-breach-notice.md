A breach notice can arrive many months after the incident it describes. A letter from Hitachi Vantara dated November 19, 2026, and filed with California’s Attorney General provides the results of a review of files accessed during an April 2025 incident. The letter gives the recipient new information about what was involved. Its date does not indicate a new intrusion.

## What the notice establishes

Hitachi Vantara says it detected suspicious server activity on April 26, 2025, reported the incident to law enforcement and brought in cybersecurity specialists. It says the review completed on June 29, 2026. The redacted notice lists personnel and identity information for that recipient, including payroll and identification details. The filing does not establish the same categories for every affected person, and it does not establish the number of recipients.

The company said at the time of the letter that it had no indication of misuse. That statement describes what the company knew when the letter was issued. It cannot establish what happens after publication.

## If you receive a notice

Compare the letter with the copy available through the regulator or confirm it with an employer contact you already know. If it applies to you, keep it and follow the guidance for the information actually listed. A notice involving identification data calls for different precautions from a notice involving an email address alone. Check account statements and watch for unexpected requests that borrow details from the incident. Verify those requests through a trusted contact rather than responding directly.
